# A 125B MoE decodes at 40.8 tok/s in an 11.35 GB footprint on a MacBook, bit-exact to its own model: likely the first exact >= 80B run under 12 GB
- Date: 2026-11-04 14:39 EET. Sessions: Fit12 (the 12 GB line, measurement), MoonShots (published-results comparison),
  orchestrator (this note).
- Claim: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (125B total, all 512 experts, ~5B active) decodes at 40.8 tok/s on 16 HumanEval prompts.
  - The engine's lifetime peak footprint is 11.35 GB on a MacBook Pro M5 Pro (64 GB machine). Expert reads use
    F_NOCACHE, so the OS page cache does not help.
  - The output is bit-exact to the line's own one-token reference (tokens and logits; checked on 4 of the 16 prompts in
    this run).
  - Experts are ~3.2 bits (ranks 0-1 at 4 bits, ranks 2-9 at 3 bits). Two-pass speculative checks, DFlash2 drafter.
- Number: 40.77 tok/s on full outputs, 49.9 tok/s on the shared text before the arms' texts diverge [K]. Lifetime peak
  phys_footprint 11.354 GB [K]. EXACT track.
- Comparison (MoonShots, research/results_first/RESULTS_FIRST.md, commit 3b3163d; every row linked there):
  - The best published exact run at <= 12 GB: SlotStream, 1.48 tok/s (4-bit, 10.7 GB process target, Mac mini M2).
  - Faster published runs near 12 GB are lossier or report only part of their memory:
    - Splash 1.4.0: 28.8 tok/s at 2.4 bits, 8.8 GB expert store on a 24 GB MacBook Air M5; total memory unknown.
    - A Splash fork: 48.8 tok/s, cache-biased (lossy) routing.
    - Palm-Infra: a 122B at 12.4 tok/s, 5.9 GiB RSS, lossy, RSS only.
  - Nothing published at >= 80B parameters and verified exact fits in <= 12 GB (or <= 24 GB).
- How: ~/Fit12/PLAN.md section 'T3R2' (the exact arm t80ck2); runs in ~/Fit12/data/scr/t3r2; log ~/Fit12/logs/summary.txt
  (13:05-13:17).
- Prior art: a result-level check (above). The methods are published or adapted (TRIED/NOVELTY.md); what is new is that
  they run exactly at this size and speed.
- Caveats:
  - Code-heavy prompts only.
  - Different precision (~3.2 b vs 4-bit / 2.4-bit). At 45 GB, the same 3-bit tiers scored the same as 4-bit on math,
    code and instructions (R-58).
  - Different hardware (M5 Pro vs M5 Air / M2).
  - The 12 GB is the engine's own footprint on a 64 GB machine, not an OS-enforced limit.
- Status: first measurement, compared against published results; not yet re-measured on mixed prompts.
